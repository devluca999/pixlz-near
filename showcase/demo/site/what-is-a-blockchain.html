What a blockchain actually is

Basics · Pixlz demo page (written for the video; not a real publication)

Strip away the jargon and a blockchain is a list of records that a lot of computers agree on. New records are added in batches, called blocks, and each block points back to the one before it.

A shared ledger

A blockchain is a shared ledger that thousands of computers keep in sync, so no single one of them can quietly rewrite the past. Changing an old record would mean redoing every block after it, on most of those computers at once.

Who decides what goes in

Each network has rules for who may add the next block and how everyone checks it. Some ask computers to burn energy solving puzzles; others, like NEAR, ask validators to put tokens at stake and lose them if they cheat.

What it is good for

Blockchains shine when people who don't trust each other need one record they can all check: who owns what, who paid whom, and when. They are a poor fit for anything that has to stay secret or change quickly.
